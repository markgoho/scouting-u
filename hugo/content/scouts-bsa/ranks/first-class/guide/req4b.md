{{< drg/requirement number="4b" text_format="html" >}}
Demonstrate how to use a handheld GPS unit, GPS app on a smartphone, or other electronic navigation system while on a campout or hike. Use GPS to find your current location, a destination of your choice, and the route you will take to get there. Follow that route to arrive at your destination.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A map and compass never run out of batteries. A GPS unit will tell you your exact position to within a few yards — but only if it has power and a clear view of the sky. This requirement isn't about replacing the skills from 4a; it's about adding a second tool and understanding what it's actually good for.

## What Counts as GPS

You can use a dedicated handheld GPS unit, a GPS app on a smartphone, or another electronic navigation device. Whatever you choose, it has to happen on a real campout or hike — not a test run in your living room.

## The Three Steps

This requirement has a specific sequence, and you need to do all three:

1. **Find your current location.** Turn on your device and confirm it has a GPS fix. Note your coordinates.
2. **Pick a destination and plan a route.** Choose a real destination — a trailhead, a summit, a campsite — and use the device to set a route or waypoint toward it.
3. **Follow the route and arrive.** Walk the route the device lays out, using it to stay on track, until you reach the destination.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Practice finding a known landmark near home before your campout. Learning how your specific device or app behaves — how it shows direction, how often it updates, how it handles losing signal under trees — is easier to do somewhere familiar than somewhere new.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
GPS satellites broadcast a signal so weak by the time it reaches the ground that it's roughly the strength of a car headlight seen from 12,000 miles away. Your receiver works by comparing tiny timing differences between signals from at least four satellites at once.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Batteries die and satellite signal drops under heavy tree cover, in canyons, or in bad weather. Never treat GPS as your only navigation tool in the field — carry a map and compass as backup, and know how to use them, on every hike where GPS is your primary method.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}
