{{< drg/requirement number="11" text_format="html" >}}
Demonstrate Scout spirit by living the Scout Oath and Scout Law. Tell how you have done your duty to God and how you have lived four different points of the Scout Law (different from those points used for previous ranks) in your everyday life.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Demonstrating Scout spirit for First Class represents a major milestone in your Scouting journey. Having previously covered four points of the Scout Law for [Tenderfoot](/scouts-bsa/ranks/tenderfoot/guide/req9/) and four different points for [Second Class](/scouts-bsa/ranks/second-class/guide/req10/), Requirement 11 asks you to explain how you have fulfilled your **Duty to God** and how you have lived the **final four remaining points of the Scout Law**—completing all twelve points across your foundational advancement!

## The Twelve-Point Milestone of the Scout Law

By the time you sit down for your First Class Scoutmaster Conference, you will have lived and reported on every single point of the Scout Law:

```
[Tenderfoot: 4 Points] + [Second Class: 4 Points] + [First Class: Final 4 Points] = ALL 12 POINTS
```

### The Twelve Points of the Scout Law
1. **Trustworthy**
2. **Loyal**
3. **Helpful**
4. **Friendly**
5. **Courteous**
6. **Kind**
7. **Obedient**
8. **Cheerful**
9. **Thrifty**
10. **Brave**
11. **Clean**
12. **Reverent**

## Fulfilling Your Duty to God

Scouting America maintains that the recognition of God as the ruling and leading power in the universe is fundamental to the best type of citizenship. The Scout Oath begins with "duty to God," and the final point of the Scout Law is "A Scout is reverent."

### Discussing Duty to God as a First Class Candidate
Be prepared to share how your personal spiritual life and moral foundation have matured:
- Participating in personal prayer, family worship, and religious services within your faith tradition.
- Practicing ethical decision-making grounded in your spiritual teachings when faced with peer pressure.
- Fulfilling service obligations through your religious institution or earning your faith's **Religious Emblems Award**.
- Practicing reverence in the backcountry—taking moments of silence to appreciate the wonder, majesty, and beauty of nature on high-adventure treks.

## Living Your Final Four Points

Identify the **four remaining points** of the Scout Law that you have not yet used for Tenderfoot or Second Class. For each point, prepare a specific, real-world experience from your recent life:

### Sample Real-Life Reflections
- **Courteous**: *"During a heated sports game when the referee made a call I disagreed with, I controlled my temper, treated the official with respect, and encouraged my teammates to keep playing hard."*
- **Obedient**: *"When our patrol leader gave instructions to pack down camp early in the rain, I followed orders immediately without complaining, helping our patrol finish on time."*
- **Brave**: *"I spoke up in defense of a classmate who was being excluded from a school group project, inviting them to partner with me despite what popular students said."*
- **Reverent**: *"I led our patrol in a respectful moment of grace before meals on our weekend campout, making sure everyone felt included regardless of their background."*

## Preparing for Your First Class Board of Review

Earning First Class is the traditional benchmark of a complete, self-reliant Scout. During your Board of Review:
- The committee will review your entire journey from Scout to First Class.
- Be prepared to discuss how living the Scout Oath and Law has changed your habits, how you support your patrol, and your goals for troop leadership and merit badges as you begin the trail to **Star**, **Life**, and **Eagle Scout**.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Review your handbook notes from all three ranks before your conference. Being able to explain how you have lived all twelve points of the Scout Law demonstrates that you are truly ready to wear the First Class badge.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
