You already know the [square knot, two half-hitches, and taut-line hitch](/scouts-bsa/ranks/tenderfoot/guide/req3a/) from Tenderfoot, and the [sheet bend and bowline](/scouts-bsa/ranks/second-class/guide/req2f/) from Second Class. Lashings are a new category entirely — instead of tying one rope, you're using rope to join wooden poles into a rigid structure.

## What You'll Complete

- 3a. **[Discuss lashing uses](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req3a/)**: Understand when lashing two poles together is the right call, and when it isn't.
- 3b. **[Tie timber and clove hitch](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req3b/)**: Learn the two hitches that start and finish every lashing you'll tie.
- 3c. **[Tie 3 lashings](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req3c/)**: Tie the square, shear, and diagonal lashings, joining two or more poles together with each.
- 3d. **[Lash a camp gadget](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req3d/)**: Put your lashings to work building something genuinely useful.

Lashings take practice to tighten properly — plan to try each one more than once before your Scoutmaster conference, ideally on a campout where you have real poles or staves to work with rather than dowels on a tabletop.
