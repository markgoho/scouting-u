## Overview

First Class is the last of the four foundational ranks, and the biggest one. It pulls together everything you built at Scout, Tenderfoot, and Second Class — camping, cooking, tools, and outdoor ethics — and adds four new skill areas: navigation with a map and compass or GPS, nature and weather awareness, aquatics, and a full first-aid and emergency-preparedness kit. Scouts who reach First Class have the complete, independent outdoor skill set the rest of the program builds on, and they're also the Scouts most likely to stay active in the program for years to come.

## Building on the Last Rank

You'll keep working the habits you started at [Second Class](/scouts-bsa/ranks/second-class/guide/): logging troop and patrol activities, living the Scout Law in your daily life, and meeting with your Scoutmaster and board of review. Two things get harder here. The activity count doubles — from 5 to 10 — and Scout spirit (requirement 11) asks for four *different* points of the Scout Law than the ones you already used, so you can't recycle your Second Class answers.

## What It Takes

First Class has no fixed tenure requirement, merit-badge count, or service-hour minimum tied to a specific number of months — how long it takes depends on your troop's schedule and how many of requirements 1-9 you can knock out on each campout. Requirement 9d does set one concrete number: three hours of service, on a project separate from the ones you already used at Tenderfoot and Second Class.

## Get Ready!

First Class asks more of you than any rank so far, but it's also where the payoff shows up fastest — you'll walk into your next campout able to plan the menu, build the fire or run the stove, tie off a real structure, navigate a course, and handle a first-aid situation, all without waiting on someone else to lead. Take it one requirement group at a time.
