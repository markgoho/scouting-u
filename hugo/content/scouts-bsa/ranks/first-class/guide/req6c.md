Identify the basic parts of a canoe, kayak, or other boat. Identify the parts of a paddle or an oar.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Understanding the anatomical terms of watercraft, paddles, and oars enables clear communication between paddlers, rowers, and leaders on the water.

## Basic Parts of a Boat

### Nautical Directional Terms
- **Bow**: The front end of the boat.
- **Stern**: The back (rear) end of the boat.
- **Port**: The left side of the boat when facing toward the bow (front).
- **Starboard**: The right side of the boat when facing toward the bow (front).
- **Forward**: Moving or looking toward the bow.
- **Aft**: Moving or looking toward the stern.
- **Amidships**: The middle section of the boat.

### Canoe Parts
- **Hull**: The curved main shell or body of the canoe that sits in the water.
- **Keel**: The longitudinal ridge running along the center bottom of the hull from bow to stern that aids in directional tracking and hull rigidity.
- **Gunwales** (pronounced *"gunnels"*): The reinforced top rails running along the upper edges of both sides of the hull.
- **Thwarts**: Wooden or aluminum crossbars connecting the gunwales across the width of the canoe that provide structural rigidity and act as bracing points.
- **Yoke**: The curved center thwart shaped to rest comfortably across a paddler's shoulders for solo portaging (carrying) the canoe.
- **Seats**: Rigid platforms located near the bow and stern for paddlers to sit or brace against while kneeling.
- **Deck plates (End decks)**: Triangular caps or decks at the extreme bow and stern tips.
- **Painter**: A painter is a 15- to 20-foot line securely fastened to the bow and stern for towing, lining, tying up to docks, or securing in emergencies.

### Rowboat and Kayak Specifics
- **Rowboat**:
  - **Transom**: The flat, vertical cross-section forming the stern of a square-ended rowboat.
  - **Transom Knee**: A structural corner bracket reinforcing the joint between the transom and the keel/gunwales.
  - **Oarlocks**: Metal or plastic U-shaped pivot sockets mounted on the gunwales that hold and pivot the oars.
- **Kayak**:
  - **Cockpit**: The central opening in the deck where the kayaker sits.
  - **Coaming**: The raised lip around the cockpit designed to secure a sprayskirt.
  - **Foot braces / pegs**: Adjustable pegs inside the hull that support the paddler's feet for powerful torso-rotation strokes.

## Parts of a Paddle and an Oar

Paddles and oars both propel watercraft, but they function differently: a paddle is held freely in the hands, while an oar is anchored to the boat in an oarlock to create a lever.

### Canoe Paddle Parts
- **Grip**: The shaped handle at the top of the paddle (commonly a palm grip or T-grip) held by the upper hand.
- **Shaft**: The long, round or oval middle section connecting the grip to the blade.
- **Throat**: The juncture or neck where the shaft tapers out and attaches to the blade.
- **Blade**: The wide, flattened lower portion that pushes against the water to generate propulsion.
- **Tip**: The bottom edge of the blade. *Tip care: Never rest a paddle on its tip or use the tip to push off rocks, sand, or docks, as this will splinter or crack the blade.*

### Oar Parts (Rowing)
- **Handle / Grip**: The rounded top end held by the rower.
- **Loom (Shaft)**: The long pole section of the oar.
- **Sleeve and Collar**: A plastic or leather protective sleeve with a raised collar ring that seats into the oarlock to prevent the oar from sliding through.
- **Blade**: The flat, flared end that enters the water.
