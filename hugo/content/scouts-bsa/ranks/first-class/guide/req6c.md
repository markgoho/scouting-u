{{< drg/requirement number="6c" text_format="html" >}}
Identify the basic parts of a canoe, kayak, or other boat. Identify the parts of a paddle or an oar.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Before you sit in a canoe or pick up a paddle for the first time, it helps to have the vocabulary — the same way you learned the parts of a knot or a compass before you used them under pressure.

## Parts of a Boat

- **Bow** — the front of the boat.
- **Stern** — the back of the boat.
- **Hull** — the boat's main body, the part that sits in the water.
- **Gunwale** (pronounced "gunnel") — the upper edge of the boat's side.
- **Keel** — a ridge running along the bottom of the hull that helps the boat track straight.
- **Thwart** — a crossbar that braces the sides of a canoe and can serve as a seat.
- **Cockpit** — the opening a paddler sits in, most relevant on a kayak.

## Parts of a Paddle or Oar

A paddle (used in canoes and kayaks) and an oar (used with a rowboat, usually mounted to the boat itself) share a similar basic shape but work differently:

- **Blade** — the flat part that pushes against the water.
- **Shaft** — the long handle connecting the blade to the grip.
- **Grip** — the top end you hold; on a canoe paddle this is often a T-grip or palm grip.
- **Throat** — where the shaft meets the blade.

An oar adds one more part a paddle doesn't have: the **oarlock**, the pivot point that attaches the oar to the boat itself, letting the rower use leverage instead of holding the full weight of the stroke.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Handle a real canoe or kayak at a troop outing or scout shop before your Scoutmaster conference — pointing to the bow, stern, and gunwale on an actual boat locks the vocabulary in far faster than reading a list.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
