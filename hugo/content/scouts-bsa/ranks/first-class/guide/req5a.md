{{< drg/requirement number="5a" text_format="html" >}}
Identify or show evidence of at least 10 kinds of native plants found in your local area or campsite location. You may show evidence by identifying fallen leaves or fallen fruit that you find in the field, or as part of a collection you have made, or by photographs you have taken.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A Scout who can name the plants around camp can also tell you which ones are safe to lean a hammock strap against, which ones make good tinder, and which ones will leave you itching for a week. Plant knowledge isn't trivia — it's part of reading the ground you're standing on.

## What Counts as "Native"

A native plant is one that grows naturally in your region without people having introduced it — as opposed to an ornamental or invasive species planted in a garden or spread from somewhere else. Your council or a local extension office can usually point you to a list of native species for your area, and many state natural resource agencies publish free regional plant guides.

## Three Ways to Show Evidence

You don't have to identify all 10 plants standing in front of a live specimen every time. This requirement allows three different kinds of evidence:

- **In the field** — point out a fallen leaf or fallen fruit and name the plant it came from.
- **A collection** — pressed leaves, seeds, or other samples you've gathered and labeled.
- **Photographs** — pictures you've taken yourself of the plant in its natural setting.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Mix your approach. Identify a few plants live on a hike, then round out the list with photos from other outings — you don't need to find all 10 species on a single walk.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/be-prepared title="You Find a Plant You Can't Identify" >}}
Don't guess and move on — a wrong identification is worse than an honest "I don't know yet."

- **Photograph it**: get a clear shot of the leaves, any flowers or fruit, and the whole plant's shape.
- **Note the location**: where you found it tells you a lot about what it might be.
- **Ask someone who knows**: a merit badge counselor for Plant Science or Mammal Study, a naturalist, or a plant identification app can help you confirm it later.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
