## Congratulations!

First Class is a real milestone — it's historically the rank where a majority of Scouts who leave the program stop, and the rank where the ones who keep going gain the full, independent outdoor skill set the rest of Scouts BSA builds on. You can now plan and cook a patrol meal, navigate with a map or GPS, read the weather, handle water emergencies, and respond to injuries — skills that don't expire when you move on to Star, Life, and Eagle. From here, the program shifts: Star, Life, and Eagle add merit badges, positions of leadership within your troop, and larger service projects, but they all assume the foundation you just finished building.

## Dig Deeper

**Beyond line rescue.** Requirement 6e taught you a shore-based line rescue for a person in the water. Look into the full "reach, throw, row, go" hierarchy that lifeguards and aquatics instructors use — line rescue is one tool in a larger decision framework about which rescue method is safest for a given situation.

**Beyond orienteering.** Requirement 4a asked you to run a course with a map and compass. Competitive orienteering is an actual sport, with timed courses through unfamiliar terrain and no marked trails — a natural next step if navigation clicked for you.

**The Scout Law, all twelve points.** Requirement 11 forced you to use whichever four points of the Law you hadn't already used at Tenderfoot or Second Class. Now that you've worked through all twelve across three ranks, look back at all of your examples together — they add up to a fuller picture of what living the Law actually looks like than any single rank's four points could show.

## Try This Next

- Take a longer trip using the navigation skills from [requirement 4](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req4a/) — a multi-leg orienteering course or a backcountry hike where GPS and a map both matter.
- Ask your troop's aquatics-certified adults what BSA Lifeguard training involves, as a next step beyond the swimmer test and line rescue from [requirement 6](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req6a/).
- Look into becoming a den chief or patrol leader — First Class is when many troops start considering Scouts for their first position of responsibility, which becomes a requirement at Star.
