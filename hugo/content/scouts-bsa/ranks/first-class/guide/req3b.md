{{< drg/requirement number="3b" text_format="html" >}}
Demonstrate tying the timber hitch and clove hitch.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Every lashing you'll tie for requirement 3c starts and ends with one of these two hitches — get them solid now, and the lashings themselves are mostly repetition of a pattern you already know.

## The Timber Hitch

The timber hitch grips a single pole tightly under a steady pull, but unlike the [taut-line hitch](/scouts-bsa/ranks/tenderfoot/guide/req3c/) you tied at Tenderfoot, it's not meant to hold that grip forever — it's designed to be started and finished quickly, which is exactly what you need at the start of a diagonal lashing.

<!-- VIDEO: timber-hitch-tying | Full tying motion for the timber hitch around a single pole -->

1. Pass the rope around the pole.
2. Tuck the working end back under the rope, then twist it around itself three or more times.
3. Pull the standing end to snug the wraps tight against the pole.

## The Clove Hitch

The clove hitch is the workhorse of lashing — it starts most lashings, and often finishes them too, because it grips a pole firmly while staying easy to tie and untie by hand.

<!-- VIDEO: clove-hitch-tying | Full tying motion for the clove hitch around a pole -->

1. Wrap the rope around the pole once.
2. Cross over the first wrap and wrap around the pole a second time.
3. Tuck the working end under the second wrap, then pull both ends to tighten.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Practice both hitches on a single pole before you try lashing two poles together in requirement 3c. If you fumble the clove hitch under pressure, the lashing built on top of it won't hold either.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The clove hitch can slip if the pole it's tied to spins freely — that's why every lashing you tie in requirement 3c finishes with extra wraps and a binding turn on top of the clove hitch, not the clove hitch alone.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}
