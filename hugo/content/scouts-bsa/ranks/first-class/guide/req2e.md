{{< drg/requirement number="2e" text_format="html" >}}
On one campout, serve as cook. Supervise your assistant(s) in using a stove or building a cooking fire. Prepare the breakfast, lunch, and dinner planned in First Class requirement 2a. Supervise the cleanup.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Serving as Head Cook for your patrol across an entire campout is a major leadership milestone. You bring all your previous preparation—your [menu](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req2a/), [budget and ingredients](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req2b/), [equipment](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req2c/), and [food safety standards](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req2d/)—into action. For this requirement, you lead the patrol kitchen, direct assistant cooks, manage stove/fire operations, and supervise thorough cleanup for Breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner.

## The Responsibilities of the Head Cook

As Head Cook, your primary responsibility is kitchen leadership and coordination:

1. **Direct Preparation:** Assign clear, specific preparation tasks to your assistant cook(s)—such as measuring ingredients, chopping vegetables, opening cans, and setting out cookware.
2. **Supervise the Heat Source:** Guide your assistant in safely lighting and operating the camp stove or building a cooking fire. If your assistant is newer to Scouting, use the **EDGE Method** (Explain, Demonstrate, Guide, Enable) rather than doing the job for them.
3. **Coordinate Timing:** Time the cooking of each dish so everything finishes at the same time. (For example: start boiling water for hot drinks and warm up the side dishes before dropping eggs onto the hot skillet).
4. **The Single-Seasoner Rule:** Only the Head Cook seasons the food. Having multiple people add salt or spices independently is the fastest way to ruin a camp meal.
5. **Enforce Kitchen Hygiene:** Ensure everyone washes hands before touching food, keeps raw meat isolated from ready-to-eat foods, and cooks meat and eggs to safe internal temperatures.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Before allowing your assistant to light a stove or fire, verify the safety perimeter: confirm the fire bucket and shovel are on hand, check fuel connections for leaks, tie back long hair, and ensure no loose clothing can catch fire.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Running the Three Meals

### Breakfast
- Wake up 15–20 minutes before the rest of the patrol to get stoves lit and hot water boiling.
- Cook the planned hot breakfast with your assistant while the rest of the patrol prepares for the day.
- Put a large pot of dishwater on the stove to heat while the patrol sits down to eat.

### Lunch
- Supervise the setup of the trail lunch or quick hot lunch.
- Keep prep time efficient so the patrol stays on schedule for afternoon activities or hikes.

### Dinner
- Allow ample time for evening meal prep (especially for Dutch oven baking or charcoal cooking, which require 45–60 minutes of coal preparation).
- Coordinate the main dish, warm sides, and dessert.

## Supervising the Three-Pot Cleanup & Kitchen Shutdown

Your job is not finished when the food is eaten. As Head Cook, you must supervise the cleanup crew to ensure the kitchen meets strict sanitation and wildlife standards:

1. **Water Heating:** Ensure wash and rinse water is heated safely during the meal.
2. **Scraping & Pre-Cleaning:** Verify that every Scout scrapes food scraps into the patrol trash bag before bringing mess kits to the wash line.
3. **Three-Pot Wash Line:** Supervise the operation of Pot 1 (Hot Soapy Wash), Pot 2 (Hot Clean Rinse), and Pot 3 (Cold Sanitizing Rinse).
4. **Air Drying:** Confirm all dishes are placed into mesh dunk bags to air-dry completely (no towels).
5. **Greywater Straining:** Supervise pouring wash water through a mesh strainer to capture solid food waste before dispersing greywater 200 feet from camp.
6. **Camp Shutdown & Wildlife Security:** Make sure all food boxes are locked, coolers are shaded and latched, cooking tables are wiped clean with sanitizer, and trash bags are secured in bear hangs, canisters, or troop trailers.
