{{< drg/requirement number="6e" text_format="html" >}}
With a helper and a practice victim, show a line rescue both as tender and as rescuer. (The practice victim should be approximately 30 feet from shore in deep water.)
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The safest water rescue is the one where the rescuer never enters the water at all. A line rescue lets you pull a struggling swimmer to safety from shore, using rope instead of your own body — and this requirement asks you to prove you can do it from both roles: tender and rescuer.

## The Setup

This requirement is done with a helper and a practice victim — someone acting the part of a swimmer in trouble, positioned roughly 30 feet from shore in deep water. You'll need a rescue line: a length of rope, ideally with a floating throw bag or a buoyant object tied to the end.

## Two Roles

- **Tender** — the person on shore anchoring the rescue. The tender braces themselves (sitting or kneeling low, with the line looped or braced against the body, never wrapped around a hand or wrist), and helps manage the line as the rescuer works.
- **Rescuer** — the person who throws or extends the line to the practice victim. Aim just beyond and slightly upstream or up-current of the victim so the line drifts into reach. Once the victim has the line, the tender and rescuer work together to pull them steadily to shore.

You have to demonstrate both roles — don't stop after doing just one.

## Reach, Throw, Row, Go

A line rescue is part of a larger hierarchy rescuers are taught: reach for the victim if possible, throw something if you can't reach, row or paddle a craft to them if throwing won't work, and only go into the water yourself as an absolute last resort, with flotation and training. The line rescue you're demonstrating here is the "throw" step — and it's why so much rescue training pushes it as the preferred method whenever it's an option.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Never swim out to a struggling swimmer without flotation and training — a panicked person can pull a would-be rescuer under. A line rescue exists specifically so you can help without putting yourself in the water. Practice this drill only under adult supervision, in a controlled swimming area.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

{{< drg/be-prepared title="The Line Falls Short or the Victim Can't Grab It" >}}
- **Coil and re-throw immediately**: don't waste time — recover the line and throw again right away.
- **Aim ahead of a moving victim**: if there's current, lead your throw so the line drifts to them.
- **Call for the next level of help**: if the line rescue isn't working, the tender should be shouting for additional help or moving to the next step in the rescue hierarchy, not repeating a failed throw indefinitely.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
