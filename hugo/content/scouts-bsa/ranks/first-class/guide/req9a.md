{{< drg/requirement number="9a" text_format="html" >}}
Visit and discuss with a selected individual approved by your leader (for example, an elected official, judge, attorney, civil servant, principal, or teacher) the constitutional rights and obligations of a U.S. citizen.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A textbook can tell you what a constitutional right is. A judge, an elected official, or a civil servant can tell you what it actually looks like to exercise, defend, or balance one in real life — which is why this requirement asks for a conversation, not research.

## Get Your Leader's Approval First

The requirement names an "individual approved by your leader" — get that approval before you set up the visit. Good options include:

- An elected official (mayor, city council member, state representative).
- A judge or attorney.
- A civil servant (police officer, firefighter, government office worker).
- A principal or teacher.

## What to Discuss

Come prepared with real questions rather than letting the conversation drift. Useful starting points:

- What rights does the U.S. Constitution guarantee to citizens, and which of those does this person's role protect or enforce?
- What obligations come with citizenship — voting, jury duty, obeying laws, paying taxes — and how does this person see people meeting or ignoring them?
- Has this person seen a case where a citizen's rights and their obligations came into tension?

{{< drg/tip >}}
Write down two or three questions before the visit. A prepared question list keeps the conversation moving and shows the person you're taking their time seriously.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

Be ready to summarize what you learned for your Scoutmaster — not word for word, but in your own understanding of what rights and obligations mean in practice.
