The menu you planned in [requirement 2a](/scouts-bsa/ranks/first-class/guide/req2a/) is just a list of dishes until you turn it into exact quantities and a real price — this requirement is where you do the math and then actually go get the food.

## Sizing the List for Three or More Youth

Take each item in your menu and work out how much of it you need to feed at least three youth — that's the floor set by the requirement, not a target to trim toward. If your patrol is bigger, scale every quantity up to match. Underestimating is the most common mistake here: a "cup of rice per person" recipe for four people needs four cups dry, not one, and it's easy to lose track of that multiplication across a whole menu.

## Building the Budget

For each ingredient, list:

- The item and the quantity needed.
- Where you'll buy it.
- Its approximate cost.

Add everything up for a total campout food budget. Compare prices where it's easy to (bulk versus individually packaged, store brand versus name brand) — a menu that looks affordable dish by dish can add up fast once you're buying for a whole patrol.

{{< drg/checklist title="Menu Budget" subtitle="One line per ingredient" >}}

- Ingredient and quantity, scaled for three or more youth.
- Where you'll buy it.
- Cost per item and running total.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Securing the Ingredients

Once your list and budget are set, actually acquire the food — buying it yourself, working with a parent, or coordinating a patrol shopping trip. "Secure the ingredients" means the food is in hand and ready to transport to the campout, not just listed on paper.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Buy perishables like meat, dairy, and produce as close to departure as you reasonably can. The longer they sit before the campout, the less cold-chain time you have left once you're in the field.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
