{{< drg/requirement number="2b" text_format="html" >}}
Describe the four steps of Scout advancement.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Advancement is the Scouting method of encouraging personal growth, self-reliance, and leadership. Every rank and merit badge you earn follows the same **four fundamental steps of Scout advancement**.

---

## The Four Steps of Scout Advancement

### 1. The Scout Learns
You acquire new skills and outdoor knowledge by doing them. You might be taught by an older Scout, a Troop Guide, a Patrol Leader, a merit badge counselor, or by studying your Scouts BSA Handbook. Learning in Scouting is hands-on and practical—pitching real tents, tying real knots, and cooking real meals.

### 2. The Scout Is Tested
When you feel confident in a skill, you demonstrate your proficiency to an authorized leader—typically an older Scout who holds the rank of First Class or above, an Assistant Scoutmaster, or your Scoutmaster. 
- You are not tested with written tests; you demonstrate the skill physically (e.g., tying a bowline around your waist, lighting a stove safely, or navigating a compass bearing).
- Once satisfied with your demonstration, the leader signs and dates your handbook.

### 3. The Scout Is Reviewed
After completing all requirements for a rank, you participate in two formal reviews:
- **The Scoutmaster Conference**: A private, one-on-one conversation between you and your Scoutmaster (held in view of other adults in compliance with Youth Protection). You discuss how you are living the Scout Oath and Law, your patrol experience, your goals for future ranks, and any challenges you face in the troop.
- **The Board of Review**: For the ranks of Tenderfoot, Second Class, First Class, Star, Life, and Eagle, a panel of **three to six adult Troop Committee members** meets with you. (The Scoutmaster and Assistant Scoutmasters do not sit on the board). The board does not retest your skills; instead, they review your journey, verify that all requirements were completed properly, ask for your feedback on how the troop is running, and confirm your character and Scout spirit.
- *(Note: Scout rank is unique—it does not require a formal Board of Review; your Scoutmaster conference completes your review step).*

### 4. The Scout Is Recognized
Once you pass your review, you receive your new rank patch at the next troop meeting so you can wear it immediately. You are then formally presented with your rank certificate and recognized before your family, fellow Scouts, and community at the next quarterly **Troop Court of Honor**.

---

## Why the Four Steps Matter

This system ensures that:
- Every Scout advances at their own individual pace.
- Standards remain consistent, fair, and objective across every troop in America.
- Scouts gain confidence by speaking with adults and being publicly celebrated for their hard work.
