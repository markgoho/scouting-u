{{< drg/requirement number="1b" text_format="html" >}}
Explain what Scout spirit is. Describe some ways you have shown Scout spirit by practicing the Scout Oath, Scout Law, Scout motto, and Scout slogan.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Scout spirit is the living embodiment of Scouting ideals. It is not something you recite; it is how you conduct yourself in everyday life—at home, in the classroom, on the athletic field, and in your neighborhood—when no one is looking or keeping score.

## What Is Scout Spirit?

Scout spirit means actively demonstrating the **Scout Oath**, **Scout Law**, **Scout Motto**, and **Scout Slogan** through your daily attitudes and actions. It is the bridge between *knowing* the words and *living* them.

A Scout with true Scout spirit does not simply put on good behavior while wearing the uniform on meeting nights. They treat the Scout Law as their personal standard of conduct seven days a week.

## Demonstrating Scout Spirit in Real Life

When preparing to discuss Scout spirit with your Scoutmaster or during a Board of Review, think about concrete, real-life examples from your everyday routine:

### At Home with Your Family
- **Helpful & Cheerful**: Doing household chores, cleaning up after dinner, or helping care for younger siblings without having to be nagged or asked multiple times.
- **Courteous & Obedient**: Respecting your parents' rules, listening attentively when family members speak, and resolving disagreements calmly.

### At School and in Sports
- **Trustworthy**: Doing your own work with academic honesty, never cheating on tests or copying assignments.
- **Friendly & Kind**: Reaching out to a classmate who is sitting alone at lunch or new to the school; encouraging teammates when they make a mistake instead of criticizing them.
- **Brave**: Standing up for someone who is being teased or bullied, and refusing to participate in gossip or hurtful social media behavior.

### In Your Patrol and Troop
- **Helpful & Thrifty**: Pitching in with fire building, cooking, and campsite cleanup on campouts without waiting for the patrol leader to assign tasks. Taking good care of troop tents and stoves to avoid damage.
- **Loyal & Cheerful**: Showing up on time to meetings and outings, supporting your patrol leader's decisions, and maintaining a positive attitude when rain or cold weather challenges the troop.

## Scout Spirit on the Trail to Eagle

Every rank advancement in Scouts BSA—from Scout to Eagle Scout—requires you to demonstrate Scout spirit. In your Scoutmaster Conferences, adult leaders will ask you to reflect on how the Scout Oath and Law have guided your decisions and character growth since your last rank.

{{< drg/tip >}}
For your Scout rank conference, prepare two specific, personal stories where you put the Scout Oath or Law into practice during the past month—one at school or home, and one during a troop activity.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
