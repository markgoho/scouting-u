Repeat from memory the Outdoor Code. List the Leave No Trace Seven Principles. Explain the difference between the two.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

**The Outdoor Code:**

> As an American, I will do my best to—
> Be clean in my outdoor manners,
> Be careful with fire,
> Be considerate in the outdoors, and
> Be conservation-minded.

**The Leave No Trace Seven Principles:**

1. Plan ahead and prepare.
2. Travel and camp on durable surfaces.
3. Dispose of waste properly.
4. Leave what you find.
5. Minimize campfire impacts.
6. Respect wildlife.
7. Be considerate of other visitors.

## What's the Difference?

Both push you toward the same goal — protecting the outdoors for the next person who visits — but they aren't the same thing. The Outdoor Code is a short pledge, written specifically for Scouts, much like the Scout Oath but aimed at how you treat the land instead of how you treat people. It states four broad values without spelling out exactly how to act on them.

Leave No Trace is a separate, more detailed framework, developed by the Leave No Trace Center for Outdoor Ethics and used by hikers, campers, and land managers well beyond Scouting. Its seven principles turn the Outdoor Code's broad ideas into specific, practical guidance — "be considerate in the outdoors" becomes concrete instructions like camping 200 feet from water or packing out every scrap of trash.

Think of the Outdoor Code as the promise, and Leave No Trace as the instruction manual for keeping it.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The Leave No Trace Center for Outdoor Ethics isn't a Scouting organization — it's an independent nonprofit, and its seven principles are used by the National Park Service, the U.S. Forest Service, and outdoor groups all over the world.
