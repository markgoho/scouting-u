{{< drg/requirement number="1d" text_format="html" >}}
Describe the First Class Scout badge and tell what each part stands for. Explain the significance of the First Class Scout badge.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You are asked to describe First Class, not Scout rank, because the First Class badge's design is the emblem used across Scouts BSA membership — it is the badge you will wear on your uniform even before you reach the rank of the same name.

## The Parts and What They Mean

- **The trefoil (three-pointed) shape** — like the north point of the compass sailors used long ago, showing that a Scout can point the right way in life as truly as a compass points north. Its three points, like the three fingers in the Scout sign, also stand for the three parts of the Scout Oath: duty to God and country, duty to others, and duty to self.
- **The eagle and shield** — the national emblem of the United States, standing for freedom and a Scout's readiness to defend that freedom.
- **The scroll** — carries the words "Be Prepared," the Scout motto, turned up at the ends to suggest a Scout's smile as he does his duty.
- **The knot at the bottom of the scroll** — reminds a Scout of the promise to do a Good Turn for someone every day, the same promise behind the Scout slogan.
- **The two stars** — stand for truth and knowledge, guiding a Scout by night and tying back to Scouting's outdoor life.

## Why It Matters

Every one of these symbols points back to something you are already learning in Requirement 1: the Oath, the motto, and the idea of belonging to something larger than one troop. The badge is a visual summary of the same values, worn where you and everyone else can see it.
