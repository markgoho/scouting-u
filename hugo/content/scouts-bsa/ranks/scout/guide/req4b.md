{{< drg/requirement number="4b" text_format="html" >}}
Show the proper care of a rope by learning how to whip and fuse the ends of different kinds of rope.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A rope with a frayed, unraveling end is a rope on its way to being useless — and a hazard, since a frayed strand can snag or fail under load without warning. Sealing the end is basic gear maintenance every Scout should know.

**Whipping** is for natural-fiber ropes (manila, sisal, cotton), which burn to ash rather than melt.
- Lay a loop of strong twine or sailmaker's thread along the rope near the cut end.
- Wrap the twine tightly around both the rope and the loop, working toward the loop's curved end.
- Keep wrapping until the **whipped band is as wide as the rope is thick**.
- Slip the working end of the twine through the loop, then pull the standing end hard to draw the loop and working end midway under the wrap to lock it in place.
- Trim the excess cord.

**Fusing** is for synthetic ropes (nylon, polyester, polypropylene), whose fibers melt and bond under heat.
- If the rope unravels easily, wrap a band of electrical tape around the spot before cutting through the tape.
- Work in a **well-ventilated area** to avoid breathing fumes.
- Hold the cut end a few inches **above** a small flame (match, lighter, or candle) rather than sticking it directly in the flame. Rotate it so the radiant heat melts the fibers evenly into a smooth, rounded cap.
- Let it cool completely before touching it — melted synthetic fibers are sticky and cause severe skin burns.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Always fuse rope with adult supervision in a well-ventilated space. Never touch molten nylon or try to shape it with your fingers while hot.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

Knowing which technique matches which rope material is the real point of this requirement — using the wrong one, like trying to fuse manila rope, won't seal the end at all.
