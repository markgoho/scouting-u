{{< drg/requirement number="6" text_format="html" >}}
Do the following:
{{< /drg/requirement >}}



This requirement is different from everything else in Scout rank. It isn't a skill or a piece of knowledge you're being tested on — it's about making sure you know how to keep yourself safe, and it's meant to be done together with a parent or guardian.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Both parts of this requirement are completed with your parent or guardian, not alone and not with your Scoutmaster. If you don't have reliable internet access at home, school, or another public place, tell your Scoutmaster — the video portion can be adjusted after talking with your parent or guardian.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Complete child protection exercises {#6a}

{{< drg/requirement number="6a" text_format="html" >}}
With your parent or guardian, complete the exercises in the pamphlet<i> How to Protect Your Children from Child Abuse: A Parent's Guide</i> .
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This pamphlet comes packaged with your Scout Handbook, and it's built to be read and discussed together with a parent or guardian rather than on your own. Set aside real time for it instead of rushing through — the exercises are meant to open a conversation, not just get checked off.

## View Personal Safety Awareness videos {#6b}

{{< drg/requirement number="6b" text_format="html" >}}
With your parent or guardian's permission, view the <i><a href="https://www.scouting.org/training/youth-protection/scouts-bsa/"> Personal Safety Awareness videos</a></i>. (See the pamphlet inserted inside the front cover.)
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

These short videos, produced by the BSA, teach age-appropriate ways to recognize situations that aren't safe, resist and get away from them, and report them to a trusted adult. That three-part idea — recognize, resist, report — is the core message to take away, even more than any specific scene in the videos.

Watch them with your parent or guardian's permission, using the link in the requirement above.

Together, these two exercises give you real tools, not just a rule to memorize: knowing what an unsafe situation looks like, trusting your own instinct to leave it, and knowing exactly who to go to afterward.
