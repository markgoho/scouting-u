{{< drg/requirement number="1c" text_format="html" >}}
Demonstrate the Scout sign, salute, and handshake. Explain when they should be used.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

These three gestures are how Scouts recognize each other and keep meetings running without a lot of shouting.

**The Scout sign** — raise your right hand, palm forward, with your thumb holding down your little finger and your other three fingers pointing up. The three raised fingers stand for the three parts of the Scout Oath (duty to others, duty to self, and helping other people); the thumb and little finger together stand for the bond between an older Scout and a younger one. Give the sign when you say the Oath, and raise it any time a leader raises theirs to call for quiet — it means "stop talking and pay attention."

**The Scout salute** — the same three fingers, but brought up to touch the brim of your hat (or your eyebrow if you're not wearing one). Use it to salute the flag during the Pledge of Allegiance and other flag ceremonies, and to greet another Scout in uniform.

**The Scout handshake** — offered with the left hand, not the right. It comes from a story about Baden-Powell meeting an African chief who greeted him by lowering his shield (normally held in the left hand) to shake hands, showing trust. Scouts use it to greet each other specifically because it is unusual enough to mark you as a Scout.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Practice the sign and salute in front of a mirror once — it's easy to get the finger positions backward the first few times.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
