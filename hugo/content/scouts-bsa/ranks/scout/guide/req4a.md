Each of these three knots does a different job, and knowing which one to reach for matters as much as knowing how to tie it.

## The Three Tests of a Good Knot

Before choosing or tying any knot, keep in mind what makes a knot effective in the field:
1. **Easy to tie** — can be formed quickly and reliably, even in the dark or cold.
2. **Stays tied** — holds its grip under expected working tension without slipping loose.
3. **Easy to untie** — can be released cleanly when the job is done without having to cut the rope.

> [!NOTE]
> Tying any knot introduces sharp bends in the fibers, reducing a rope's overall breaking strength by **35% to 55%**. Dressing the knot cleanly (smoothing and arranging the strands so they lie parallel without unnecessary twists) maximizes strength and safety.

## The Three Basic Knots

- **Square knot** — joins two ropes of the same thickness. It lies flat and is ideal for tying bandage ends in first aid, securing packages or gear rolls, and finishing off packages.
  - *Caution*: If the working ends are crossed the wrong way, you create a "granny knot" or "thief knot," which will easily slip or capsize under load.
  - *Not for heavy loads*: Never use a square knot for critical load-bearing, rescues, or joining ropes of different diameters.

- **Two half-hitches** — anchors a rope to a fixed post, tree, grommet, or ring.
  - *How it works*: The rope passes around the post, and two identical half-hitches are tied around the standing part in the **same direction**.
  - *Strength*: It holds firmly under steady pull but loosens easily by hand when tension is released.

- **Taut-line hitch** — a friction-based sliding hitch that grips firmly under tension but slides freely by hand when adjusted.
  - *Where to use*: Guy lines for tents, dining flies, and tarp tie-outs.
  - *How it works*: Make two wraps around the standing line **inside** the loop (toward the peg or tree), followed by one half-hitch **outside** the loop in the same direction. This allows you to tighten a sagging tent line in seconds without untying it.
