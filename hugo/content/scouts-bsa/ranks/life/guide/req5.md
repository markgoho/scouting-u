Leadership at the Life Scout rank requires increased dedication, accountability, and maturity. While Star required four months, Life extends the leadership commitment to **six full months** of active service in an approved position of responsibility.

## Approved Youth Leadership Roles in a Scout Troop

The following positions of responsibility are officially approved by Scouting America for the Life rank:

- **Senior Patrol Leader (SPL)**: Elected by the troop. Plans and conducts troop meetings, leads the Patrol Leaders' Council (PLC), coordinates with the Scoutmaster, and oversees troop operations.
- **Assistant Senior Patrol Leader (ASPL)**: Appointed by the SPL. Second-in-command; oversees troop meeting flow, directs troop logistics, and guides appointed youth officers.
- **Patrol Leader (PL)**: Elected by the patrol. Fosters patrol identity, represents the patrol on the PLC, coordinates campsite setup and meal duty rosters on campouts.
- **Troop Guide**: Appointed by the SPL and Scoutmaster. Mentors new Scouts, assists the new-Scout patrol leader, and teaches scoutcraft skills through First Class.
- **Order of the Arrow Troop Representative (OA Rep)**: Coordinates OA elections, promotes lodge and chapter fellowships, and leads unit conservation service.
- **Scribe**: Maintains accurate troop attendance records, tracks dues, records PLC meeting minutes, and handles administrative communications.
- **Quartermaster**: Manages the troop trailer, tents, stoves, tools, and equipment; tracks checkouts, repairs, and inventory.
- **Historian**: Preserves troop history, manages photo archives, maintains digital scrapbooks, and produces annual unit displays.
- **Librarian**: Maintains the troop library of merit badge pamphlets and literature; oversees checkout records.
- **Webmaster**: Manages the troop website, digital calendar, email newsletters, and electronic platforms under Youth Protection guidelines.
- **Chaplain Aide**: Coordinates non-denominational Scouts' Own services on campouts, promotes the 12th point of the Scout Law (Reverent), and provides peer encouragement.
- **Outdoor Ethics Guide**: Trains the troop in Leave No Trace principles, Tread Lightly! ethics, and the Outdoor Code.
- **Junior Assistant Scoutmaster (JASM)**: For Scouts at least 16 years old. Acts as a youth assistant Scoutmaster, mentoring junior leaders and assisting adult leadership.
- **Bugler**: Sounds musical bugle calls at meetings, ceremonies, flag raisings, and campouts. *(Approved for Star and Life, but not for Eagle).*
- **Instructor**: Instructs Scouts in specific scouting, first aid, pioneering, and outdoor skills.
- **Den Chief**: Assists a Cub Scout den leader with weekly den meetings, activities, games, and outdoor events.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
**Assistant Patrol Leader (APL) is NOT an approved position of responsibility** for rank advancement (as noted in Footnote 9 and *Guide to Advancement* topic 4.2.3.4.4).
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

## Venturing, Sea Scout, and Lone Scout Roles

- **Venturing Crew**: President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, Den Chief, Historian, Guide, Quartermaster, Chaplain Aide, or Outdoor Ethics Guide.
- **Sea Scout Ship**: Boatswain, Boatswain's Mate, Purser, Yeoman, Storekeeper, Crew Leader, Media Specialist, Specialist, Den Chief, or Chaplain Aide.
- **Lone Scout**: Significant leadership responsibility in school, religious organization, club, or community organization.

## What "Active Service" Requires

To meet the standard of serving actively:
1. **Understand Expectations**: Review the written duties of your role with your Scoutmaster or SPL at the start of your six-month term.
2. **Execute Consistently**: Regularly attend troop meetings, PLC meetings, and campouts, actively carrying out your duties throughout the six months.
3. **Continuous Term or Combined Roles**: You may serve six months in a single role or combine time across multiple approved roles (e.g., three months as Patrol Leader followed by three months as Troop Guide).

## The Scoutmaster-Approved Leadership Project

If no approved position is available in your unit, your Scoutmaster may assign a substantial **leadership project** to help the troop:
- The project must involve real leadership, planning, and execution over an extended duration.
- It must be formally agreed upon and approved in advance by your Scoutmaster.
- *(Note: A leadership project is allowed for Star and Life, but is NOT permitted for the Eagle Scout rank).*

{{< drg/tip >}}
Leadership is about serving others. As a Life Scout leader, focus on empowering newer Scouts, delegating effectively, and fostering positive morale across your patrol and troop.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
