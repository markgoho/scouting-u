{{< drg/requirement number="2" text_format="html" >}}
As a Life Scout, demonstrate Scout Spirit by living the Scout Oath and Scout Law. Tell how you have done your duty to God, how you have lived the Scout Oath and Scout Law in your everyday life, and how your understanding of the Scout Oath and Scout Law will guide your life in the future. List on your Eagle Scout Rank Application the names of individuals who know you personally and would be willing to provide a recommendation on your behalf, including parents/guardians, religious (if not affiliated with an organized religion, then the parent or guardian provides this reference), educational, employer (if employed), and two other references.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You've told the Scout spirit story at every rank since Star, most recently at [Life](/scouts-bsa/ranks/life/guide/req2/). Eagle asks for it one more time, but changes the shape of the answer: instead of a conversation you prepare for, this requirement asks for a written statement, and it adds something entirely new — a list of people who will vouch for you on your Eagle Scout Rank Application.

## The Written Statement

Your statement covers three things, and each one asks a different question:

- **Duty to God, past tense**: an actual choice or moment from your own faith tradition or beliefs where you did your duty to God — specific and personal, not a general claim of belief.
- **Living the Oath and Law, past tense**: real moments from your everyday life, since your last few ranks, where a point of the Law or the Oath's promise to do your best shaped a decision you made.
- **Living the Oath and Law, future tense**: this is new at Eagle — how your understanding of the Oath and Law will guide choices you haven't made yet. Earlier ranks only ever asked about the past.

{{< drg/tip >}}
If you've kept notes since your last board of review, you already have the raw material for the past-tense sections. The future-tense section is the one you can't look up — give it real thought instead of a one-line placeholder.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

## The References List

Separately from the statement, this requirement asks you to list, on your Eagle Scout Rank Application, people who know you personally and are willing to give a recommendation on your behalf:

- A parent or guardian.
- A religious reference — or, if you aren't affiliated with an organized religion, your parent or guardian provides this reference instead.
- An educational reference (a teacher, counselor, or school official).
- An employer, if you're employed.
- Two other references of your choosing.

No earlier rank has asked you to name references. Contact each person before you submit your application — a reference who's caught off guard by a call from your council slows your Eagle board of review down, and it's your responsibility to give them a heads-up, not your council's.

{{< drg/checklist title="Before You Submit Your References" subtitle="Confirm each one before your application goes in" >}}

- Parent or guardian named and aware.
- Religious reference named and aware, or parent/guardian standing in for it.
- Educational reference named and aware.
- Employer named and aware, if you have one.
- Two additional references named and aware.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

This requirement is graded on whether your statement is honest and specific to your own life, and whether your references are real people who can actually speak to who you are — not on which points of the Law you happen to mention or how impressive your references sound on paper.
