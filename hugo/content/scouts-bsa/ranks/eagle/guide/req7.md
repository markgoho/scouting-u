{{< drg/requirement number="7" text_format="html" >}}
Successfully complete your board of review for the Eagle Scout rank. <br><br> In preparation for your board of review, prepare and attach to your Eagle Scout Rank Application a statement of your ambitions and life purpose and a listing of positions held in your religious institution, school, camp, community, or other organizations, during which you demonstrated leadership skills. Include honors and awards received during this service. (This requirement may be met after age 18, in accordance with Guide to Advancement topic 8.0.3.1.)
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This is your last board of review — the same format you've known since Tenderfoot, a short meeting with a small panel of adults, but this one confirms the highest rank in the program.

## A Second, Different Statement

Requirement 2 asked for a statement about your duty to God and how the Oath and Law have shaped you. This requirement asks for something separate: a written statement of your ambitions and life purpose, plus a listing of leadership positions you've held in your religious institution, school, camp, community, or other organizations, including any honors and awards you received. Attach both to your Eagle Scout Rank Application. Don't reuse your requirement 2 statement here — the board is looking for two different things, not one essay doing double duty.

## What the Board Confirms

The board is confirming that requirements 1 through 6 actually happened: six real months as an active Life Scout, Scout spirit lived out and put in writing, 21 merit badges with 13 from the required list, six months in an approved position of responsibility, a service project you planned and led, and a Scoutmaster conference that covered all of it.

## If You're Approaching 18

Everything for Eagle — including this board of review — normally has to be completed before your 18th birthday. Two exceptions exist:

- An Eagle board of review can happen without special approval within 24 months after your 18th birthday, as long as every other requirement was finished before you turned 18.
- If a permanent or long-term disability affects your ability to complete requirements, alternative provisions exist through your council's advancement committee — this requires a special application, submitted well before you turn 18, not after.

A time extension past these limits is rarely granted and reserved for circumstances genuinely outside your control — it is not a backup plan for starting Eagle requirements late.

## If the Board Doesn't Approve

As at every earlier rank, boards of review almost always end in approval, since the requirements should already be confirmed before the board meets. If your board doesn't approve your advancement, you can request a board of review under disputed circumstances, and if that board still doesn't approve you, you can appeal the decision through your council — both processes are laid out in the BSA *Guide to Advancement*.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="Your 18th Birthday Is Close and Requirements Are Still Open" >}}
You realize with a few months left that a merit badge, your service project, or your board of review itself won't be finished before you turn 18.

- **Talk to your Scoutmaster immediately**: don't wait until the deadline is a matter of weeks — the sooner an adult who knows the process is involved, the more options stay open.
- **Check whether every other requirement is truly done**: the after-18 board of review window only applies if everything except the board itself was finished before your birthday.
- **Don't assume an extension will be granted**: extensions are rare and reserved for real, unavoidable circumstances, not for requirements you simply didn't get to in time.
- **Keep working**: a board of review after 18 is still a real path to Eagle — treat it as the plan, not a last resort you're hoping to avoid needing.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
If anything in your record is unclear — a counselor's sign-off, your project's final approval, a reference who hasn't responded — resolve it before the board meets, not during it.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

Once the board is satisfied, you'll be notified that you've earned Eagle Scout — the highest rank Scouts BSA offers.
