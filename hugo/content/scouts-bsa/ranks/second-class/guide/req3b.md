{{< drg/requirement number="3b" text_format="html" >}}
Using a compass and map together, take a 5-mile hike (or 10 miles by bike) approved by your adult leader and your parent or guardian.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This is the hike everything else in requirement 3 was building toward — five miles on foot, or ten by bike, actually using the compass and map skills from requirement 3a instead of just following whoever's in front of you.

## Getting Approval First

Before you go, the route needs approval from both your adult leader and your parent or guardian — get sign-off from both before you set out, not after.

## Using the Map and Compass Along the Way

Bring your oriented map and compass and actually reference them during the hike: take a bearing at the trailhead, check your position against the map at trail junctions, and confirm your direction of travel periodically rather than only at the start. The goal is a hike where you could explain, at any point, roughly where you are and which way you're headed.

{{< drg/checklist title="The 10 Essentials" subtitle="Pack all ten for every hike" >}}

- Pocketknife.
- First-aid kit.
- Extra clothing, for weather-appropriate layers.
- Rain gear.
- Water bottle, filled for the full distance plus a margin.
- Flashlight.
- Trail food, a snack or lunch appropriate for the hike length.
- Matches and fire starters.
- Sun protection.
- Map and compass.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

Share your approved route with someone not on the hike before you leave, in addition to what you're carrying.

If a wheelchair, crutches, or a mobility difference makes hiking difficult for you, this requirement and requirement 3c allow a "trip" in place of a "hike" — talk with your Scoutmaster about an appropriate alternative before you plan your route.
