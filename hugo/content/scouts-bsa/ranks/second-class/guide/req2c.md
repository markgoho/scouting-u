{{< drg/requirement number="2c" text_format="html" >}}
Using a minimum-impact method at an approved outdoor location and time, use the tinder, kindling, and fuel wood from Second Class requirement 2b to demonstrate how to build a fire. Unless prohibited by local fire restrictions, light the fire. After allowing the flames to burn safely for at least two minutes, safely extinguish the flames with minimal impact to the fire site. Properly dispose of the ashes and any charred remains.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

This is where the wood you cut in requirement 2b becomes an actual fire — built, lit, watched burn for at least two minutes, then fully put out and cleaned up, leaving the site no worse than you found it.

## Building the Fire

Use an existing fire ring whenever one is available, rather than building a new fire scar. Lay your tinder loosely at the center, add kindling in a shape that lets air flow through — a teepee or log-cabin stack both work — and keep your fuel wood staged within reach but not blocking the airflow.

## Lighting and Watching It Burn

Light the tinder and feed kindling in gradually as it catches. The requirement calls for at least two minutes of safe burning once the fire is going — use that time to watch it, not walk away from it. A cooking fire needs constant attention; that's part of what this requirement is checking.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Keep a water source or dirt within reach the entire time the fire is burning, never leave it unattended, and confirm current fire restrictions before you light anything — if restrictions are in effect, skip the lighting step, as the requirement itself allows.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Putting It Out Completely

- Drown the fire with water, stirring the ashes so every ember gets wet.
- Repeat until the ashes are cool enough to touch with the back of your hand held just above them.
- Scatter any cooled ashes and charred remains, or pack them out if the site doesn't allow scattering.

A fire isn't truly out until it's cold to the touch — a fire that looks out but is still smoldering underneath is one of the most common causes of accidental wildland fires.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="The Fire That Won't Fully Die" >}}
You've soaked the ashes twice, but you can still feel heat when you hold your hand above them.

- **Stir again**: break up any clumped ash so water reaches every ember.
- **Add more water**: don't assume one round is enough for a well-established fire.
- **Recheck with the back of your hand** held just above the ashes, not by touching directly.
{{< /drg/be-prepared >}}
