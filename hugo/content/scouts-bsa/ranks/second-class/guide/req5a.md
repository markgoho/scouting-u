{{< drg/requirement number="5a" text_format="html" >}}
Tell what precautions must be taken for a safe swim.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Every organized BSA swim follows the same safety framework, called Safe Swim Defense — it's the reason a troop swim looks different from a casual trip to the pool with friends.

## The Core Precautions

- **Qualified supervision**: an adult trained in Safe Swim Defense oversees every swim.
- **Physical fitness**: swimmers should have no known health conditions that make swimming unsafe, and evidence of fitness is on file for the activity.
- **Safe area**: the swimming area is checked for hazards — depth, currents, underwater obstructions — and marked into zones for non-swimmers, beginners, and swimmers.
- **Lifeguards on duty**: trained lookouts and lifeguards watch swimmers at all times.
- **Lookout**: a person is specifically assigned to watch for trouble across the whole area, separate from the lifeguards.
- **Ability groups**: swimmers are classified as non-swimmer, beginner, or swimmer, and each group stays within its designated area.
- **Buddy system**: every swimmer has a buddy, and buddies check in together on a regular signal.
- **Discipline**: everyone follows the rules of the swim area without exception.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Safe Swim Defense applies to every BSA swimming activity, not just formal swim tests. These precautions exist because drowning can happen silently and fast — the buddy system and lookout exist specifically to catch that before it becomes an emergency.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Why It Matters Before You Get In

Reciting this list matters less than understanding why each piece exists — a swim area without a lookout, or buddies who wander off from each other, is exactly how small problems turn into real emergencies. Be ready to explain not just what the precautions are, but what each one is protecting against.
