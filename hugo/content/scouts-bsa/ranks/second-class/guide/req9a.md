{{< drg/requirement number="9a" text_format="html" >}}
Explain the three R's of personal safety and protection.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You [viewed BSA's Personal Safety Awareness videos as a Scout](/scouts-bsa/ranks/scout/guide/req6b/). This requirement asks you to explain the framework behind that training: the three R's.

## The Three R's

- **Recognize**: know what situations, behaviors, or requests aren't okay — someone asking you to keep an uncomfortable secret, unwanted touching, or a person pressuring you to break a rule for their benefit.
- **Resist**: it's okay to say no, loudly and clearly, even to an adult or someone in authority. You have the right to refuse.
- **Report**: tell a trusted adult right away — a parent, a Scout leader, a teacher — even if you're told to keep it secret. A secret that makes you uncomfortable is exactly the kind of secret worth telling.

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
No one — not another Scout, not an adult, not a leader — has the right to make you feel unsafe or to ask you to keep a secret that involves your safety. Recognizing that clearly is the first R for a reason: you can't resist or report something you don't first recognize as wrong.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Why This Framework Matters

The three R's give you a simple, memorable sequence to fall back on under pressure, when clear thinking is hardest. Be ready to explain each one in your own words and describe the kind of situation where you'd use it — not just recite the three words.
