{{< drg/requirement number="1c" text_format="html" >}}
On one of these campouts, select a location for your patrol site and recommend it to your patrol leader, senior patrol leader, or troop guide. Explain what factors you should consider when choosing a patrol site and where to pitch a tent.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A campsite that looks fine at 3 p.m. can turn into a lake at 3 a.m. — a low spot that seemed flat and shady can pool with runoff the moment it rains. Reading the ground before anyone unpacks is a skill that saves the whole patrol a miserable night.

## What Makes a Good Patrol Site

- **Level, well-drained ground.** Avoid low spots and dry creek beds — they flood first when it rains.
- **Distance from hazards.** Stay away from dead trees or large dead branches ("widowmakers") that could fall, and away from cliff edges or unstable slopes.
- **At least 200 feet from water sources and trails**, following Leave No Trace guidance to protect the shoreline and keep the path clear for other hikers.
- **Existing, durable surfaces.** A previously used site protects untouched ground from new impact — this is [Leave No Trace's second principle](/scouts-bsa/ranks/second-class/guide/req1b/) in practice.
- **Reasonable distance from the kitchen and latrine areas**, so smells and traffic don't run through your sleeping area.

## Where to Pitch a Tent Within the Site

Once you've picked the general area, look for a level spot without rocks or roots underneath, angled so that any rain runs away from the tent rather than under it. Check the ground above your spot too — water traveling downhill will find your tent if you're camped in its path.

## Making the Recommendation

Walk the site with the factors above in mind, then bring your recommendation to your patrol leader, senior patrol leader, or troop guide — explain *why* you picked the spot, not just where it is. A good recommendation names the specific hazards you checked for and ruled out.

{{< drg/be-prepared title="The Spot That Looks Perfect" >}}
You find a flat, grassy clearing that looks ideal for the patrol site — but it sits in a shallow dip below the trail.

- **Check the drainage**: look for signs of standing water, matted-down grass, or a dry streambed nearby.
- **Look uphill**: if rain runoff from higher ground would funnel toward this spot, keep looking.
- **Choose higher, sloped ground instead**, even if it's slightly less flat — a dry tent beats a level one.
