Demonstrate how a compass works and how to orient a map. Use a map to point out and tell the meaning of five map symbols.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A map tells you where things are. A compass tells you which way you're facing. Neither one is much use for real navigation without the other — this requirement is about connecting them.

## How a Compass Works

A baseplate compass has a magnetized needle that always points toward magnetic north, a rotating bezel marked with degrees, and a clear baseplate for laying against a map. To take a bearing, you turn the bezel to line up the direction you want to travel, then turn your whole body until the needle lines up inside the bezel's outline — a technique often called "boxing the needle" or "putting Fred in the shed."

## Orienting a Map

To orient a map, lay it flat and lay your compass on top with the direction-of-travel arrow pointing toward the top of the map. Turn the map and compass together until the compass needle lines up with north on the map. Once oriented, everything on the map now points the same direction as the real terrain around you — a hill drawn to your left on the map is a hill to your actual left.

## Reading Map Symbols

Topographic maps use a standard set of symbols to pack a lot of information into a small space. Pick five symbols from your map and be ready to point them out and explain what each one means — common examples include contour lines (elevation change), a blue line (a stream or river), a trail marker, a building icon, or a symbol for a water source.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Practice orienting a map and taking a bearing in your own backyard or a local park before your hike. It's much easier to learn the motion somewhere familiar than to figure it out for the first time on the trail.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
