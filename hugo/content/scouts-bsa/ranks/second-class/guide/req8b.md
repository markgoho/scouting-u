{{< drg/requirement number="8b" text_format="html" >}}
Explain what respect is due the flag of the United States.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The American flag is not merely a piece of decorated cloth; it is the living emblem of the United States of America. It represents the Constitution, the rule of law, national unity, and the sacrifices of generations of veterans and citizens. Second Class Requirement 8b asks you to explain the foundational principles of respect codified in the federal **United States Flag Code** (Title 4, United States Code).

---

## Core Principles of Flag Respect

Under the U.S. Flag Code, the flag represents a living country and is itself considered a living thing. The following rules demonstrate that respect in everyday handling and formal ceremonies:

### 1. Never Dip the Flag
The United States flag is **never dipped** (lowered forward) to any person, monarch, president, or insignia. While state, military unit, and organization flags dip in salute to the national standard, the American flag always remains held high and erect.

### 2. Never Display the Flag Upside Down
The flag should never be displayed with the Union (blue star field) down, **except as a signal of dire distress** in instances of extreme danger to life or property.

### 3. Never Let the Flag Touch the Ground
The flag should never touch anything beneath it—the ground, a floor, water, or merchandise. If the flag accidentally brushes the ground, it does not have to be destroyed; simply pick it up immediately, brush off any dirt, and handle it with renewed care.

### 4. Never Carry the Flag Flat
The flag should never be carried flat or horizontally (such as across an entire football field), but always carried aloft, upright, and free to ripple in the breeze.

### 5. Never Use the Flag as Apparel, Drapery, or Costume
- The flag should never be used as clothing, athletic uniforms, bedding, cushions, costumes, or drapery. (A formal embroidered flag patch worn on a Scout uniform, military uniform, or astronaut suit is a proper mark of patriotism).
- For decorative patriotic draping at podiums or stages, use **red, white, and blue striped bunting** (with the blue stripe on top), never an actual flag.

### 6. Never Deface or Mark the Flag
No mark, insignia, letter, word, figure, design, picture, or drawing of any nature should ever be placed upon, attached to, or embroidered onto any part of the American flag.

### 7. Never Use the Flag for Commercial Advertising
The flag should never be used in any manner for advertising purposes, printed on disposable paper napkins, candy wrappers, paper plates, or commercial packaging intended to be discarded in the trash.

### 8. Respectful Retirement of Worn Flags
When a flag becomes worn, frayed, soiled, or torn to the point where it is no longer a fitting emblem for display, it should be destroyed in a dignified and reverent manner—preferably by **burning in a solemn retirement ceremony**.

## Respect During Ceremonies and Anthems

Whenever the flag passes in a parade, during flag raising/lowering, or when reciting the Pledge of Allegiance or singing the National Anthem:
- **Scouts in Uniform**: Stand at attention, face the flag, and render the **Scout salute** from the first note/command until the last.
- **Civilians and Scouts in Activity Wear**: Stand at attention, remove any hats with the right hand, and place the **right hand over the heart**.
- **Moving Vehicles**: When the flag passes in a procession, all individuals present halt and face the colors.

{{< drg/tip >}}
When speaking with your Scoutmaster, explain that respect for the flag is not about worshipping an object—it is about honoring the freedoms, rights, and people the flag represents.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
