{{< drg/requirement number="8b" text_format="html" >}}
Explain what respect is due the flag of the United States.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You already know *how* to display, raise, lower, and fold the flag from Tenderfoot. This requirement is about *why* — the respect behind those actions, spelled out under the US Flag Code.

## Respect in Everyday Handling

- The flag should never touch the ground, floor, or water.
- It should never be used as clothing, drapery, or a covering for anything except a casket.
- It should be displayed only from sunrise to sunset unless properly illuminated at night.
- It should never be flown in bad weather unless it's an all-weather flag.
- A worn or damaged flag should be retired, traditionally by burning in a dignified ceremony, not thrown in the trash.

## Respect During a Ceremony

When the flag passes by in a parade or ceremony, or during the raising or lowering of the flag, everyone present stands, faces the flag, and salutes or places their right hand over their heart. Scouts in uniform give the Scout salute; others use the hand-over-heart salute.

{{< drg/did-you-know >}}
The tradition of never letting the flag touch the ground isn't about avoiding a dirty flag — it's a mark of respect so deeply rooted that even an accidental touch traditionally calls for a moment's pause to lift and re-fold it properly.
{{< /drg/did-you-know >}}

Be ready to explain not just the rules, but why they exist: the flag represents the country itself, so how it's handled is treated as a direct reflection of the respect shown to what it stands for.
