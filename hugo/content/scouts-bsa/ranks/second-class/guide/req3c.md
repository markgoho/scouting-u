{{< drg/requirement number="3c" text_format="html" >}}
Describe some hazards or injuries that you might encounter on your hike and what you can do to help prevent them.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The best time to think about what could go wrong on your hike is before you're five miles from the trailhead, not after something happens.

## Hazards to Watch For

- **Terrain hazards**: loose rock, exposed roots, stream crossings, and steep or eroded sections of trail.
- **Weather exposure**: sudden temperature drops, rain, or sun exposure over a long distance.
- **Traffic**, if any part of your route follows a road or crosses one.
- **Dehydration and blisters**, the two most common injuries on a hike of this length.
- **Wildlife or insects**, depending on your local terrain.

## Preventing Each One

- **Watch your footing** on loose or uneven ground, and slow down on stream crossings and steep sections rather than rushing them.
- **Check the forecast beforehand** and pack layers so weather changes don't catch you unprepared.
- **Walk facing traffic on roads without a sidewalk**, single file, as far to the side as possible.
- **Drink water steadily rather than only when thirsty**, and break in properly fitted footwear before a 5-mile hike, not on the day of it.
- **Know what's native to your area** — poison ivy, ticks, or specific wildlife — and how to avoid contact.
