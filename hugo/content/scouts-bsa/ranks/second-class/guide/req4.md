{{< drg/requirement number="4" text_format="html" >}}
Identify or show evidence of at least 10 kinds of wild animals (such as birds, mammals, reptiles, fish, or mollusks) found in your local area or camping location. You may show evidence by tracks, signs, or photographs you have taken.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Most wild animals hear or smell you long before you see them — so a lot of real wildlife identification isn't spotting the animal itself, it's reading the evidence it left behind.

## What Counts as an Animal

The requirement's own examples cover a wide range: birds, mammals, reptiles, fish, or mollusks. Ten different species across any mix of those categories satisfies the requirement — you don't need ten mammals or ten birds specifically.

## Three Ways to Identify an Animal

- **Direct sighting**: seeing the animal itself, even briefly.
- **Tracks**: footprints in mud, sand, or snow, which often identify a species even when you never see the animal.
- **Signs**: other evidence an animal leaves — scat, fur caught on a branch, feathers, a chewed pinecone, a nest, or a burrow.
- **Photographs you have taken**: a picture you personally took of the animal, its tracks, or its signs also counts as evidence.

## Building Your List

Keep a simple running list as you go on outings — species name, how you identified it (sighting, track, sign, or photo), and where. A local field guide or a wildlife identification app can help you confirm a track or sign you're not sure about.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Early morning and dusk are the most active times for most wildlife. If you're struggling to reach ten species, plan a short walk near camp right after sunrise or just before dark.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
