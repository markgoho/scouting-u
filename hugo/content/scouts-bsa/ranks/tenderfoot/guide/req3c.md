{{< drg/requirement number="3c" text_format="html" >}}
Demonstrate a practical use of the taut-line hitch.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The taut-line hitch — the third knot from [Scout requirement 4a](/scouts-bsa/ranks/scout/guide/req4a/) — earns its keep on exactly one job: holding a line taut while still letting you adjust that tension by hand, without ever untying it.

## A Practical Use

The classic application is guying out a tent or tarp:

1. Tie the taut-line hitch around the main line, a short distance from the stake.
2. Slide it to tighten or loosen the guy line as needed.
3. Once it's snug, the hitch grips the main line and holds — even overnight, even in wind.

## Why It Beats a Fixed Knot

A fixed knot like a clove hitch holds its tension the moment you tie it — if the rope stretches or the wind shifts the tent, you have to retie it. The taut-line hitch's extra wraps let it slide under light pressure but bite down under load, so you can re-tension a guy line in seconds as the night gets colder and your rope contracts.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Rope stretches and tents settle overnight. Check your taut-line hitches again the next morning — they may need to be slid tighter after the rope relaxes.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
