{{< drg/requirement number="7a" text_format="html" >}}
Demonstrate how to display, raise, lower, and fold the American flag.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The American flag carries a set of etiquette rules that go well beyond "hang it up somewhere." Getting them right is a small but real way of showing respect for what the flag represents.

## Displaying

- Never let the flag touch the ground, floor, or water.
- When displayed flat against a wall or in a window, the union (blue field of stars) goes to the observer's left.

{{< drg/image src="images/flag-display-union-position.png" alt="Photo of the American flag displayed flat against a wall with the union correctly positioned to the observer's left" >}}
- When flown with other flags on the same halyard, the U.S. flag goes at the peak, or on its own right if flags are side by side.

## Raising and Lowering

- Raise the flag briskly to the top of the pole.
- Lower it slowly and ceremoniously.
- The flag is typically flown from sunrise to sunset unless it's properly illuminated after dark.

## Folding

The flag is folded into a triangle, star field showing, through a series of repeated triangular folds starting from the striped end. Practice this with a partner — one person holds one end while the other folds — since it's difficult to do cleanly alone.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Practice the fold with an old or practice flag first. Folding a flag incorrectly in front of a group is a common — and easily avoidable — first-time mistake.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
A flag that's torn, faded, or otherwise no longer fit for display should be retired respectfully — typically by burning in a dignified ceremony — never thrown in the trash. Ask your Scoutmaster about your troop's flag retirement procedures.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
