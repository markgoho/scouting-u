{{< drg/requirement number="7a" text_format="html" >}}
Demonstrate how to display, raise, lower, and fold the American flag.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

The flag of the United States of America represents the nation's history, unity, and constitutional ideals. Respect for the flag is governed by the federal **United States Flag Code** (Title 4, United States Code). To complete Requirement 7a, you must demonstrate the proper protocols for displaying, raising, lowering, and folding the flag.

## 1. Rules for Displaying the Flag

{{< drg/image src="images/flag-display-union-position.png" alt="Photo of the American flag displayed flat against a wall with the union correctly positioned to the observer's left" >}}

### Basic Care & Ground Clearance
- **Never Touch the Ground**: The flag should never touch the ground, floor, water, or any merchandise beneath it.
- **Weather Conditions**: The flag should not be flown in inclement weather (heavy rain, snow, or high winds) unless using a certified, all-weather synthetic flag.
- **Hours of Display**: The flag is customarily displayed outdoors from **sunrise to sunset**. If flown 24 hours a day, it must be properly illuminated with a dedicated spotlight throughout the night.

### Display Orientations
- **Flat Against a Wall (Horizontal or Vertical)**: The **Union (the blue field of stars)** must always be at the top and to the **flag's own right**—which is the **observer's upper left**, whether the flag hangs horizontally or vertically.
- **Crossed Staffs with Another Flag**: When displayed with another flag on crossed staffs (such as a state or troop flag), the U.S. flag is placed on its own right (observer's left), and its staff must be on the outside—**crossing in front of** the other staff.
- **Grouped with State or Organization Flags**: When a group of flags is displayed on staffs, the U.S. flag should be at the **center and at the highest point** of the group.
- **Speaker's Platform or Stage**: When displayed on a speaker's platform, the flag is placed on a stand to the **speaker's right** (the audience's left as they look at the stage).

## 2. Raising and Lowering Protocols

Flags must be handled with deliberate ceremony and respect:

- **Hoisting**: Raise the flag **briskly** (smartly and quickly) to the peak of the flagpole.
- **Lowering**: Lower the flag **slowly, solemnly, and ceremoniously**.
- **The Color Guard**: The flag should be carried folded to the pole. The halyard (rope) is attached while the flag is held securely by two Scouts, preventing it from brushing against the ground.

### The Half-Staff Protocol (National Mourning)
Flying the flag at half-staff is a national symbol of mourning ordered by the President or state Governor:
1. **Raising to Half-Staff**: First, hoist the flag **briskly all the way to the peak for an instant**, then slowly lower it to the half-staff position (roughly halfway down the staff).
2. **Retiring from Half-Staff**: At sunset (or at the end of the ceremony), first hoist the flag **briskly to the peak again**, and only then lower it slowly and respectfully all the way down.

## 3. Step-by-Step Guide to Folding the Flag

Folding the flag requires **two Scouts** working as a synchronized team:

1. **Hold Taut**: Both Scouts hold the flag waist-high, parallel to the ground, stretched flat between them.
2. **First Lengthwise Fold**: Fold the lower striped section lengthwise over the upper section, bringing the edges together.
3. **Second Lengthwise Fold**: Fold the flag lengthwise a second time, ensuring the blue star field remains on the outside.
4. **Begin Triangular Folding**: The Scout at the striped end starts folding a corner into a neat right-angle triangle against the open edge.
5. **Continue Triangles**: Fold the triangle inward along the open edge to create a second triangle, repeating this process along the entire length of the flag.
6. **Tuck the Tail**: When only the blue field remains at the end, neatly fold and tuck the remaining blue fabric into the pocket formed by the triangular folds.
7. **Final Form**: The finished fold should be a crisp, tight triangular shape (resembling a colonial "cocked hat") with **only the blue field and white stars visible**.

When retiring a worn, frayed, or faded flag that is no longer fit for display, it should not be discarded in the trash. Ask your Scoutmaster about participating in a respectful, dignified troop **Flag Retirement Ceremony**, where flags are formally retired by burning in a ceremonial campfire.
