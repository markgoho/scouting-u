{{< drg/requirement number="4d" text_format="html" >}}
Assemble a personal first-aid kit to carry with you on future campouts and hikes. Tell how each item in the kit would be used.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A first-aid kit only helps if it matches the injuries you're actually likely to face — which is exactly what requirements 4a and 4b just covered. A personal kit handles scrapes, blisters, and other minor injuries; a larger patrol or home kit is what you lean on for more serious emergencies. Building your own kit, instead of grabbing a generic one off a shelf, is what makes this requirement stick.

{{< drg/checklist title="Personal First-Aid Kit" subtitle="The Scouts BSA Handbook list — fit it all in a resealable plastic bag" >}}

- 6 latex-free adhesive bandages, assorted sizes: for small cuts and scrapes.
- 2 sterile 3-by-3-inch gauze pads: to cover larger wounds or apply pressure to stop bleeding.
- A small roll of latex-free adhesive tape: to hold gauze in place, or to tape over a hot spot.
- A 3-by-6-inch piece of moleskin: cut to size to pad hot spots and blisters.
- A small bar of soap or small bottle of alcohol-based hand-sanitizing gel: to clean your hands and the wound area before treating.
- A small tube of triple antibiotic ointment (optional): to help keep a cleaned wound from getting infected.
- A small tube of hydrocortisone cream (optional): to calm itching from insect bites and plant rashes.
- Scissors: to cut tape, gauze, and moleskin.
- Tweezers: for splinters and ticks.
- Disposable latex-free gloves: to protect both you and the person you're treating.
- CPR breathing barrier: to give rescue breaths safely if someone stops breathing.
- Pencil and paper or a small notebook: to record what happened, the time, and the treatment given — useful for adult leaders or emergency responders.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Some people are allergic to antibiotic ointments and hydrocortisone cream. Always ask permission before applying either one to someone else.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

## Tell How You'd Use Each Item

The requirement doesn't just ask you to pack a kit — it asks you to explain each item's purpose. Go through your kit item by item and connect it back to a specific injury from requirement 4a: which item treats a blister, which one you'd reach for on a tick bite, and so on.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Pack everything in a resealable plastic bag so it stays dry, and check it before every trip — replace anything you've used and swap out anything that's expired. Add any personal medication you need, clearly labeled.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

Size your kit to fit in your pack without adding much weight. A first-aid kit that's too bulky to carry ends up left at home — which defeats the purpose.

If you have a smartphone, ask your parent or guardian about downloading the American Red Cross's free First Aid app. It includes videos, step-by-step instructions for common injuries, and quizzes to test your knowledge.
