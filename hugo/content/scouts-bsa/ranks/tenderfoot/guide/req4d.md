{{< drg/requirement number="4d" text_format="html" >}}
Assemble a personal first-aid kit to carry with you on future campouts and hikes. Tell how each item in the kit would be used.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A first-aid kit only helps if it matches the injuries you're actually likely to face — which is exactly what requirements 4a and 4b just covered. Building your own kit, instead of grabbing a generic one off a shelf, is what makes this requirement stick.

{{< drg/checklist title="Personal First-Aid Kit" subtitle="A starting point — build yours to match your own trips" >}}

- Adhesive bandages, several sizes: for cuts and scrapes.
- Sterile gauze pads and adhesive tape: for larger wounds.
- Moleskin or blister pads: for hot spots before they become blisters.
- Antiseptic wipes or antibiotic ointment: to clean wounds before covering them.
- Tweezers: for splinters and ticks.
- Disposable gloves: to protect both you and the person you're treating.
- Any personal medication you or a buddy might need, labeled clearly.
- A small first-aid reference card or folded instruction sheet.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Tell How You'd Use Each Item

The requirement doesn't just ask you to pack a kit — it asks you to explain each item's purpose. Go through your kit item by item and connect it back to a specific injury from requirement 4a: which item treats a blister, which one you'd reach for on a nosebleed, and so on.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Keep your kit in a small, waterproof pouch or bag, and check it before every trip — replace anything you've used and swap out anything that's expired.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

Size your kit to fit in your pack without adding much weight. A first-aid kit that's too bulky to carry ends up left at home — which defeats the purpose.
