{{< drg/requirement number="2b" text_format="html" >}}
While on a campout, demonstrate an appropriate method of safely cleaning items used to prepare, serve, and eat a meal.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Dirty dishes in the backcountry aren't just gross — a poorly cleaned pot is how a whole patrol ends up with stomach trouble on day two. This requirement is about doing dishwashing right, not just doing it.

## The Three-Pot Method

Most troops use a version of this simple system:

Scrape food scraps into the trash first (never on the ground) — this keeps the wash pot from getting overwhelmed with loose food.

1. **Wash.** Scrub in a first pot of hot, soapy water using biodegradable soap.
2. **Rinse.** Dip in a second pot of clear, hot water to remove soap residue.
3. **Sanitize.** Dip in a third pot of cold water with a few drops of bleach or a sanitizing tablet (like Steramine), then air-dry on a clean surface.

{{< drg/image src="images/three-pot-dishwashing-method.png" alt="Diagram of the three-pot dishwashing method: wash, rinse, and sanitize pots in a row" >}}

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Never dump greywater (dishwater) directly into a stream, lake, or drainage — strain out food solids and scatter the water at least 200 feet from any water source, following your site's specific rules.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Let dishes air-dry instead of wiping them with a shared towel — a damp towel passed around the patrol can spread germs faster than the dishes ever would.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

Demonstrate this — or whatever method your troop actually uses — for your leader on an actual campout. Talking through the steps isn't enough; you need to show the process with real dishes.
