{{< drg/requirement number="4c" text_format="html" >}}
Tell what you can do while on a campout or other outdoor activity to prevent or reduce the occurrence of injuries or exposure listed in Tenderfoot requirements 4a and 4b.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Knowing how to treat an injury is only half the skill — the other half is not needing to. This requirement asks you to flip requirements 4a and 4b around: instead of "how do I fix this," it's "how do I avoid this in the first place."

## Prevention, Injury by Injury

- **Cuts, scrapes, and blisters**: Wear broken-in boots, keep a knife sharp (a dull blade slips), and change out of wet socks before they rub raw.
- **Burns and scalds**: Keep a clear "kitchen zone" around the stove or fire, and never reach across an open flame.
- **Insect and tick bites**: Use insect repellent, tuck pants into socks in tick habitat, and do a tick check every evening.
- **Venomous snakebite**: Watch where you place your hands and feet, especially near rocks and logs, and never handle a snake you can't positively identify as harmless.
- **Nosebleeds**: Stay hydrated and avoid rough contact sports without supervision; dry air and dehydration make nosebleeds more likely.
- **Frostbite and sunburn**: Dress in layers and cover exposed skin in the cold; use sunscreen and a hat, and reapply sunscreen after swimming or heavy sweating.
- **Choking**: Slow down while eating, especially around a campfire in the dark, and cut food into manageable pieces.
- **Hazardous plants**: Learn to identify them before you go (see requirement 4b), and stay on established trails where they're less likely to grow.

Notice the pattern: almost every prevention step here is about awareness — knowing your gear, your surroundings, and your own body — rather than any special equipment. That's the point of this requirement: most outdoor injuries are preventable with attention, not luck.
