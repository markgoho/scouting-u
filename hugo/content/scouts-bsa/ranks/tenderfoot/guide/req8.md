{{< drg/requirement number="8" text_format="html" >}}
Describe the steps in Scouting's EDGE method. Use the Teaching EDGE method to teach another person how to tie the square knot.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

EDGE is how Scouts BSA expects you to teach anything, from a knot to a whole merit badge — and this is the first rank where you're asked to use it, not just watch someone else use it on you.

## The Four Steps

{{< drg/checklist title="Scouting's EDGE Method" subtitle="Four steps, always in this order" >}}

- **E**xplain: Describe the skill and why it matters, in words.
- **D**emonstrate: Show the skill yourself, step by step.
- **G**uide: Let the learner try it while you coach and correct.
- **E**nable: Step back and let them do it on their own, ready to help if asked.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

Each step hands more control to the learner. Skipping straight from "Explain" to "Enable" — just describing a knot and expecting someone to tie it — is the single most common way EDGE goes wrong.

## Teaching the Square Knot

Since you already mastered the [square knot for requirement 3a](/scouts-bsa/ranks/tenderfoot/guide/req3a/), you're well-positioned to teach it. Walk your learner through all four steps in order, and resist the urge to just tie it for them when they struggle — the "Guide" step is where the real learning happens, and it works best when you let them make (and fix) their own mistakes.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Pick a learner who genuinely doesn't already know the square knot. Teaching someone who's faking confusion doesn't give you honest practice with the Guide and Enable steps.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
