{{< drg/requirement number="8" text_format="html" >}}
Describe the steps in Scouting's EDGE method. Use the Teaching EDGE method to teach another person how to tie the square knot.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Leadership in Scouting begins with the ability to teach others effectively. The **EDGE method** is Scouting's proven, four-step instructional framework used by youth and adult leaders across all training courses. Requirement 8 asks you to describe all four steps and use them to teach another person how to tie the square knot.

## The Four Steps of the EDGE Method

```
[EXPLAIN] ➔ [DEMONSTRATE] ➔ [GUIDE] ➔ [ENABLE]
```

### EXPLAIN (Talk Through the Concept)
- Describe the skill clearly in words.
- Explain **why** the skill is important and when it is used.
- Break the process down into manageable, logical steps without overwhelming the learner with technical jargon.

### DEMONSTRATE (Show the Skill)
- Perform the skill slowly and deliberately in front of the learner.
- Position yourself so the learner has the same viewpoint (e.g., sitting side-by-side rather than facing backwards).
- Narrate your actions as you perform them, pointing out key details and common pitfalls.

### GUIDE (Coach While They Practice)
- Hand the materials to the learner and have them attempt the skill.
- Provide immediate, positive encouragement and gentle corrections as they work.
- **Resist the temptation to take over!** If they struggle, guide them with questions ("Which working end goes over first?") rather than doing the step for them.

### ENABLE (Step Back for Mastery)
- Step back and allow the learner to perform the skill completely independently.
- Have them execute the task successfully several times without assistance or prompting.
- The learner has now mastered the skill and is enabled to use it in camp or teach it to someone else.

## Putting EDGE into Practice: Teaching the Square Knot

Follow this exact sequence to teach the square knot:

### Step 1: Explain
*"Today we are going to learn how to tie a square knot (also called a reef knot). It is used to join two ropes of equal thickness or to tie packages and first-aid bandages. The rule to remember is: 'Right over left and under, then left over right and under.'"*

### Step 2: Demonstrate
Hold two rope ends. Tie the first half-knot ("Right over left and under"). Show how it lays flat. Then tie the second half-knot ("Left over right and under"). Pull it tight and show the completed knot: two interlocking loops that slide smoothly along each other without jamming. Show how a wrong cross creates an insecure "granny knot."

### Step 3: Guide
Hand the ropes to your learner. Walk them through each half-knot step-by-step. If they cross the wrong end on the second step, gently point out that the loops won't interlock properly and coach them to reverse the cross.

### Step 4: Enable
Have the learner untie the rope completely, scramble the ends, and tie the square knot independently two or three times until they can do it smoothly without hesitation.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Teach someone who does not already know the knot—such as a younger sibling, parent, or a new Scout. Teaching a genuine beginner gives you authentic practice working through the Guide and Enable steps.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
