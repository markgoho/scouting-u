{{< drg/requirement number="1b" text_format="html" >}}
Spend at least one night on a patrol or troop campout. Sleep in a tent you have helped pitch.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

Pitching your own tent is what turns "I have a tent" into "I can shelter myself." A tent set up wrong sags, lets in rain, or collapses at 2 a.m. — so this requirement is really about self-reliance, not just fabric and poles.

## Before You Pitch

- **Pick a flat, dry site** clear of rocks, roots, and low spots where water pools.
- **Check for hazards overhead** — dead branches ("widow-makers") can fall in wind or rain.
- **Orient the tent** with the door away from prevailing wind when you can.

## Pitching Steps

1. Lay out the footprint or ground cloth first, sized to (not larger than) the tent floor.
2. Assemble poles and thread them through their sleeves or clip them to the tent body.
3. Stake out the corners first, then the rainfly, pulling guy lines taut but not overstretched.
4. Check that the rainfly doesn't touch the tent body anywhere — contact points let rain soak through.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Practice pitching your tent in the backyard before the campout. The first time you do it should not be in the dark, in the rain, at the campsite.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

{{< drg/safety-first >}}
Never pitch a tent under a dead or damaged tree, near a fire ring's downwind side, or in a low spot that could flood in heavy rain.
{{< /drg/safety-first >}}

This requirement is satisfied by the doing, not by talking about it — you need at least one full night's sleep in a tent you helped set up. Be ready to describe what you did to pitch it when your leader or a fellow Scout asks.
