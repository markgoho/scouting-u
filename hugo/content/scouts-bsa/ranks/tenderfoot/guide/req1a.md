{{< drg/requirement number="1a" text_format="html" >}}
Present yourself to your leader, prepared for an overnight camping trip. Show the personal and camping gear you will use. Show the right way to pack and carry it.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

A poorly packed backpack turns a fun hike into a shoulder-aching slog. Getting your gear right — and packed right — before you even leave the driveway is the first skill every Scout has to master, because nobody can fix a bad pack job once you're a mile down the trail.

## Personal Gear vs. Group Gear

Personal gear is yours alone: clothing, sleeping bag, mess kit, first-aid kit, rain gear. Group gear — tents, cook sets, water filters — usually gets split among patrol members so no one person carries all of it. Ask your patrol leader what you're responsible for carrying before you pack.

{{< drg/checklist title="Overnight Camping Gear" subtitle="A typical personal gear list for one night out" >}}

- Backpack: sized to fit everything below without overstuffing.
- Sleeping bag and sleeping pad: rated for the season's expected low temperature.
- Tent (if not assigned as group gear) or your share of a shared tent.
- Extra clothing layers: rain gear, warm layer, extra socks.
- Mess kit: plate, bowl, cup, utensils.
- Personal first-aid kit (see requirement 4d).
- Water bottle or hydration system.
- Headlamp or flashlight with spare batteries.
- Toiletries: toothbrush, toothpaste, small towel.
{{< /drg/checklist >}}

## Packing It Right

- **Heavy items go low and close to your back.** This keeps your center of gravity stable and stops the pack from pulling you backward.
- **Frequently used items go on top or in outer pockets** — rain gear, snacks, map — so you're not unpacking the whole bag on the trail.
- **Everything gets a place.** A packing list only helps if you use the same layout every trip; that consistency is what lets you find things fast in the rain or in the dark.

{{< drg/image src="images/packed-backpack-layout.png" alt="Cutaway diagram of a loaded backpack with heavy items packed low and close to the back, and frequently used items in top and outer pockets, each area labeled" >}}

{{< drg/tip >}}
Do a full practice pack at home before the campout, then wear the loaded pack around the block. You'll find balance problems and forgotten items while you can still fix them.
{{< /drg/tip >}}

When you present your gear to your leader, be ready to explain *why* each item is in your pack, not just that it's there. That's what shows you understand the gear, not just that you can copy a list.
