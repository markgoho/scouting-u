{{< drg/requirement number="4b" text_format="html" >}}
Describe common poisonous or hazardous plants; identify any that grow in your local area or campsite location. Tell how to treat for exposure to them.
{{< /drg/requirement >}}

You can't avoid a plant you can't recognize. This requirement asks you to learn the hazardous plants that actually grow where you camp — not a generic national list, but the ones you'll really encounter.

## Common Hazardous Plants

- **Poison ivy, oak, and sumac** cause an itchy, blistering rash from an oil called urushiol on the leaves and stems. "Leaves of three, let it be" is a starting point, not a complete identification — sumac, for example, has more than three leaflets.
- **Stinging nettle** causes an immediate stinging or burning sensation on contact with fine hairs on the leaves and stems.
- **Poison hemlock and giant hogweed** are more serious: hemlock is toxic if ingested, and hogweed sap can cause severe burns when it contacts skin in sunlight.

## Research Your Local Area

Which of these actually grows where you camp varies a lot by region. Ask your Scoutmaster, check a regional plant guide, or look up your state extension office's hazardous plant list — then be ready to identify at least one that shows up at your own campsites.

## Treating Exposure

- **For poison ivy/oak/sumac**: Wash the area with soap and cool water as soon as possible — within 10 minutes if you can — to remove the oil before it binds to skin. A mild rash can be treated with calamine lotion or hydrocortisone cream; a severe reaction, especially near the eyes or face, needs medical attention.
- **For stinging nettle**: The sting usually fades on its own within an hour; cool water or a baking soda paste can ease the discomfort.
- **For hemlock or hogweed contact**: Wash the area immediately and seek medical attention — these are more serious than a simple rash.

{{< drg/tip >}}
Learn to recognize these plants in every season, not just summer. Poison ivy without leaves in winter still carries urushiol on its bare stems.
{{< /drg/tip >}}
